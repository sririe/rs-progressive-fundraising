# Post-Meeting Action Plan

## Objective

Keep momentum with Progressive after the March 13, 2026 website recommendations meeting by converting a positive response to the discovery proposal into a scheduled on-site session and a tighter internal plan.

The priority is to move from:

- a discovery-first recommendation centered on digital gift card workflows

to:

- a confirmed discovery engagement with clear scope, team roles, and client-facing next steps

## What Happened In The Meeting

### What Red Stamp put forward

The deck and Spencer's framing positioned the next step as a **structured discovery engagement**, not a full solution recommendation. The core argument was:

- gift cards should be treated like cash equivalents
- the current process and any future portal carry meaningful security risk
- Red Stamp should not finalize a build recommendation before validating Lloyd's workflow and current security boundaries
- the right first step is a half-day on-site discovery session producing vendor runbooks, a technical specification, and a security assessment

The pricing/timing presented in the meeting was:

- **40 hours total effort**
- **$6,750 investment**
- deck positioned for **April 2026**

### How the client responded

Doug reacted positively to the discovery concept and reinforced that he sees Red Stamp as a long-term technology partner. He also expanded the problem space beyond the digital workflow that anchored the presentation.

Three active pain areas surfaced:

1. **Digital cards**
   The original anchor of the pitch. Still important, but Progressive leadership is not fully aligned on how far they want to go into end-recipient delivery versus keeping responsibility with the client contact.

2. **Physical-card stickering**
   Doug raised this early and framed it as the first stage in a broader operations automation roadmap.

3. **Invoicing / admin process streamlining**
   Doug identified this as another current friction point that Red Stamp should consider in the overall technology roadmap.

### Key strategic signal from the client

Doug and Gord appear interested in a broader advisory relationship, but they are still testing the boundaries of what should actually be built. There is real appetite for progress, but also caution about overbuilding workflows that may create more liability than value.

The biggest example is **end-recipient digital delivery**:

- Doug sees it as a possible value add for larger digital clients
- Gord is not yet convinced it is a real enough pain point to justify the complexity and liability
- Tim reinforced that this is several steps beyond Progressive's current infrastructure

This means the meeting did not expand the scope of the immediate recommendation so much as it surfaced a second decision Red Stamp must manage carefully:

- what belongs in the discovery engagement
- what belongs in later roadmap options

## Recommended Position After The Meeting

### Keep the main recommendation intact

Red Stamp should continue to anchor the immediate opportunity around **digital gift card fulfillment discovery**.

That remains the cleanest entry point because it:

- matches the reasoning in the deck
- maps to the original operational fragility around Lloyd's workflow
- has a concrete and already-socialized price/time frame
- unlocks future work with better evidence

### Do not ignore the scope expansion

The meeting clearly introduced additional operational problems that matter to Progressive:

- stickering of physical cards
- invoicing and administrative workflow

These should be acknowledged and captured, but not allowed to derail the discovery engagement into a vague all-things-operations audit.

### Best framing

The most practical follow-up framing is:

> The proposed discovery remains focused on the digital gift card workflow because that is where the highest security risk and the greatest fulfillment fragility sit today. During that discovery, we can also capture and preliminarily assess the adjacent operational pain points you raised, especially stickering and invoicing, so we can recommend what should be tackled next and in what order.

This keeps the pitch disciplined while showing that Red Stamp listened.

## Discovery Scope Recommendation

### Core scope: stay fixed

The discovery engagement should still center on:

- current vendor-by-vendor digital fulfillment workflows
- Lloyd's scripts and handoffs
- Mario transferability / operator readiness
- current card-data movement and storage
- security boundaries and risk profile
- technical specification for the next digital system

### Expanded scope: include as bounded assessment inputs

The adjacent topics raised in the meeting should be included as **lightweight assessment areas**, not equal-weight deliverables:

- physical-card stickering workflow
- invoicing / QuickBooks-related administrative workflow

The goal is not to fully solve those during discovery. The goal is to:

- understand the current-state friction
- gauge business value and urgency
- identify whether either one should become the next recommended initiative after digital fulfillment

### What not to let discovery become

The discovery should not quietly become:

- a full operational transformation engagement
- an open-ended process audit across the whole business
- a commitment to build end-recipient distribution
- a commitment to integrate directly with merchant platforms

Those may become later phases, but the post-meeting follow-up should keep them explicitly outside the immediate engagement unless re-scoped and re-priced.

## Immediate Internal Actions

### 1. Lock the follow-up narrative

Owner: Spencer

Prepare a short follow-up note to Doug and Gord that:

- thanks them for the meeting
- confirms Red Stamp heard the three areas of friction
- keeps the proposed discovery anchored on digital gift cards
- states that stickering and invoicing can be included as bounded inputs to the discovery
- aims to schedule the on-site session in **March 2026** if calendars allow

### 2. Reconcile timing

Owner: Stephanie with Spencer and Danny

The deck asked for an April session, but Doug explicitly requested March because he will be away in April. This needs to be corrected in the next client communication.

Action:

- identify March on-site options for Danny
- confirm whether Spencer joins fully remote or partially on site
- send date options quickly while meeting momentum is fresh

### 3. Confirm discovery package boundaries

Owner: Spencer and Danny

Before the next client email goes out, align internally on whether the existing discovery package remains:

- **40 hours**
- **$6,750**
- half-day on site
- runbooks + technical spec + security assessment

Recommended default:

- keep the package intact
- describe stickering and invoicing as additional assessment inputs within the same engagement only if they do not materially change effort
- if they do materially change effort, treat that as a scoped revision rather than absorbing it informally

### 4. Prepare the fielding team

Owner: Spencer

Core initial team based on current needs:

- Spencer: engagement lead, client narrative, synthesis, technical recommendation framing
- Danny: Director of Operations, on-site lead, workflow capture, operational assessment
- Stephanie: scheduling, follow-ups, action tracking, client coordination
- Tim: technical risk review, systems constraints, security-informed implementation thinking
- Bronte: implementation feasibility support, workflow translation into build implications

Design should be looped in later, once the likely next build phase is clearer.

### 5. Capture the open strategic question explicitly

Owner: Spencer

Document internally that Progressive leadership is still undecided on this question:

- Is direct delivery to individual end recipients a strategic service Progressive actually wants to own, or is it an appealing capability that introduces more operational burden and liability than it is worth?

This should be treated as a discovery question, not an assumed roadmap item.

## Proposed Client-Facing Follow-Up

### Near-term message

Send a concise email that does four things:

1. confirms the discovery-first recommendation
2. reflects back the additional pain points raised in the meeting
3. clarifies that the session can include those as part of prioritization
4. proposes March dates for the on-site session

### Suggested talking points

- We came away from the meeting even more convinced that the right first step is a structured discovery focused on the digital gift card workflow.
- We also heard clearly that stickering and invoicing are important operational pain points.
- Our recommendation is to keep the discovery centered on the digital workflow, while using that session to capture and assess those adjacent areas so we can advise on what should come next.
- The immediate goal is still the same: document the workflow, assess the security posture, and come back with a recommendation you can trust.

## Questions To Resolve Before Or During Discovery

These are the key questions the meeting surfaced and that the follow-up should keep visible:

1. Can the on-site session happen in March 2026, before Doug leaves in April?
2. Does the existing 40-hour / $6,750 package still hold if stickering and invoicing are included as assessment inputs?
3. How much of Progressive's actual client demand truly points toward end-recipient digital distribution?
4. Is Mario now sufficiently independent that the workflow transfer risk has materially changed since January?
5. How should Red Stamp distinguish between:
   - documenting adjacent operational pain points
   - and committing to solve them in the same engagement?

## Practical Next Step

The next move should be simple:

- send the follow-up note
- offer March dates
- keep the discovery package focused
- use the on-site session to earn the right to widen the roadmap, rather than widening it prematurely in email

That is the best way to preserve momentum without losing scope discipline.
