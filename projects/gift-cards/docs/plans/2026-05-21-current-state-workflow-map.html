Deeper context - current state

How digital fulfillment works today

This maps the observed order-to-delivery path from discovery. It is meant to help the team see where people, systems, card data, and handoffs create risk.

Main issue

The workflow is not one system.

It is a chain of paper, email, QuickBooks, Formidable Forms, Google Drive, scripts, merchant files, SystemOne, and Gmail.

Trigger

Payment confirmation starts fulfillment.

The practical trigger is a hard copy invoice stamped paid and reconciled, not a digital queue.

Recommendation link

This is why the vault comes first.

The vault can stabilize the fulfillment path before replacing the ordering experience or adding customer self-service.

Observed workflow

1. Order placedCustomer / website or email

Customer places an order through the current website forms or a legacy email order path.

Formidable Forms captures some information, but business rules still need human review.

2. Invoice createdElena / Mario / QuickBooks

Progressive creates the invoice, handles discount or tax details, and sends it to the customer.

QuickBooks remains the system of record for invoicing.

3. Payment confirmedElena / paper handoff

Payment is confirmed, the hard copy is stamped paid and reconciled, and the invoice returns to the fulfillment desk.

This is the current fulfillment trigger.

4. Order preparedMario / Google Drive / scripts

The operator opens Lloyd's folder structure, runs scripts against the invoice, and creates merchant-specific working files.

Errors still route back to the person who understands the scripts.

5. Cards fulfilledMerchant-specific

The operator pulls URLs or files, generates PDFs from card numbers, or activates Walmart cards just in time.

The steps vary by merchant and are not all captured in one place.

6. DeliveredGmail / SystemOne / encrypted files

Progressive sends card files, URL lists, or encrypted ZIPs, plus a separate password email.

SystemOne is used when generated PDFs need hosted URLs.

Where the process gets fragile

Area What happens today Why it matters
Fulfillment trigger Paid orders are triggered by a physical invoice returning to the fulfillment desk. No shared queue means work can depend on one person's desk, email habits, or memory.
Digital inventory Cards and URLs live across merchant spreadsheets, folders, staging files, and generated outputs. Cash-equivalent data is hard to track across its full life cycle.
Merchant steps Some merchants provide URLs, some require generated PDFs, and Walmart has a separate activation flow. Training a replacement requires more than one generic runbook.
Support When scripts fail, the team reconstructs what happened from folders, files, and whoever remembers the process. Support is slower and harder to delegate.
Customer experience Customers receive delivery emails and files, with limited order history or self-serve retrieval. Portal improvements need a stable fulfillment foundation underneath them.
