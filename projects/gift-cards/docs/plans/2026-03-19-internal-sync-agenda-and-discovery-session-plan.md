# Notes for Internal Sync and Discovery Session

## My read on where things stand

My takeaway from the March 13 call is that the conversation went well.

Doug seemed positive about the discovery-first approach, and I think the bigger signal was that he wants us to help shape what they should do, not just react to a list of features. That part felt encouraging to me.

At the same time, the conversation widened a bit. We went in anchored on the digital gift card workflow, and that still feels like the right center of gravity. But Doug also brought up stickering and invoicing, which are clearly real pain points for them.

My view is that we should acknowledge those and make room to capture them, but not let this first step turn into a fuzzy all-of-operations engagement. If we do that, I think we lose the clarity that made the discovery recommendation compelling in the first place.

The other practical thing is timing. The deck pointed to April, but Doug asked if we could do the on-site in March because he will be away in April. So to me, one of the main jobs now is just keeping momentum and getting that scheduled while the conversation is still fresh.

## What I want us to cover in the 30-minute internal sync

### 0:00-0:05 | Quick readout on the client call

- what seemed to land well
- where the conversation expanded
- what felt encouraging vs. what still feels unresolved

### 0:05-0:12 | Reconfirm the shape of the recommendation

- are we still aligned that discovery should stay anchored on digital fulfillment
- do we still feel good about not broadening this into a larger ops engagement yet
- does the current 40-hour / $6,750 framing still feel right

### 0:12-0:18 | Get crisp on scope

- what absolutely needs to be covered in the on-site session
- what we want to capture lightly as adjacent input
- what we should be careful not to imply is included

### 0:18-0:24 | Decide the next client-facing move

- do we want to send a quick follow-up right away
- do we want to get March dates first
- who do we need in the room on their side for the session to be worthwhile

### 0:24-0:30 | Owners and immediate next steps

- who owns scheduling
- who owns shaping the session
- who owns the follow-up note
- what needs to happen before we start drafting a statement of work

## What I'd like us to leave that meeting aligned on

- discovery is still digital-first
- stickering and invoicing are in the conversation, but not driving the whole engagement
- we know whether the current package still holds as-is
- we know exactly what we're asking Progressive for next

## My current view of what the on-site session needs to do

I don't think we need the session to answer everything. I do think we need it to give us enough real-world detail that we're no longer making important assumptions about how their digital workflow actually works.

For me, the session should give us three things:

- a clear picture of the vendor-by-vendor workflow today
- a grounded sense of where the real security and handoff risks are
- enough clarity to recommend what the next build step should be, and what should wait

## What I think we need to uncover while we're there

### 1. How the digital workflow actually works today

- how card data comes in for each vendor
- what Lloyd and Mario actually do step by step
- which tools, scripts, spreadsheets, portals, or manual workarounds are involved
- where things get slow, brittle, or error-prone

### 2. Where the sensitive data lives and moves

- where card data exists at each point in the process
- who can access it
- how it gets transferred or stored today
- what security protections are already in place around URLs and fulfillment

### 3. How transferable the process really is

- what still lives mostly in Lloyd's head
- how independently Mario can actually run the workflow today
- what would still be hard for a non-technical operator to take over

### 4. What Progressive really wants to own on the delivery side

- whether end-recipient delivery is actually a near-term need or just an attractive idea
- what their clients are really asking for
- what turnaround expectations matter in practice

### 5. The adjacent pain points, at a lighter level

- high-level understanding of the stickering workflow
- high-level understanding of invoicing / QuickBooks friction
- enough context to say whether either one should be the next priority after digital

## Rough shape of the half-day session

This does not need to be rigid, but this is roughly how I think the time should be used.

### Start with framing

- align on what we're there to understand
- make it clear that digital fulfillment is the main focus
- make room to capture stickering and invoicing without letting them take over

### Spend most of the time on the actual digital workflow

- walk vendor by vendor
- look at the real tools and steps, not just a verbal summary
- note exceptions, edge cases, and places where things break

### Then focus on security and handoffs

- trace where the risky moments are
- understand current controls
- identify where a future system would need to be especially careful

### Then look at operator readiness

- compare Lloyd knowledge with Mario's actual operating independence
- identify what is undocumented
- identify what a tool or runbook would need to simplify

### Finish with adjacent ops inputs and closeout

- get a practical read on stickering and invoicing
- confirm what still needs follow-up afterward
- make sure we leave with the artifacts and notes we need

## Prework I think would help

- a list of the active digital vendors
- sample inputs and outputs for each workflow
- any existing notes, checklists, or runbooks they already have
- clarity on who should be in the room for which parts
- any constraints around screenshots, recording, or sharing files

## Guardrails I'd want us to keep in mind

- stay anchored on digital fulfillment
- don't accidentally let discovery become a broad transformation engagement
- capture stickering and invoicing, but keep them in the right place
- leave with clarity on who owns each risky handoff today

## The simplest way I'd frame it internally

The meeting went well. The discovery recommendation still feels right. The main thing now is to keep the scope disciplined, get the March session scheduled, and use that session to replace our assumptions with something real.
