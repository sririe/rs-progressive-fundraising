## Draft Email

Hi Doug,

Thanks for the forward — hope you're getting some real downtime out there.

Happy to evaluate this and jump on a call with them when you're back. From what you shared, it sounds like the gap is on the distribution side — they want to move from the physical cards we used to send them to something that gets cards into their recipients' hands without all the work in the middle landing on them. That's a real ask, and it lines up well with conversations the team has been having internally over the last couple of weeks, so the timing is good.

A few things would help us walk into the call useful rather than exploratory:

1. **What did they order from us before, and at what scale?** Roughly which merchants, what denominations, how many cards — that tells us whether we're in familiar territory or whether they're asking for something we'd be standing up for the first time.
2. **What does "exactly what they need" actually look like in their words?** Sending cards directly to each recipient, a portal where their admin can manage orders, reporting on which cards were used, all of the above? "Distribution help" can mean a few different things and it shapes the rest of the conversation.
3. **Who are the recipients?** End consumers, employees, program participants? Each one has different deliverability and support implications, and that's the part Gord and I want to be careful around.
4. **Is there a date they're working toward?** Q4 holiday volume is always our real constraint; if they're aiming at the holiday season, that shapes what we say yes to.

One thing I want to flag in advance, so we can all walk into the call with the same picture:

Sending cards directly to recipients on their behalf is technically buildable. It's also a different operating posture for Progressive than what you do today. Today Progressive delivers to the buyer, and the buyer distributes from there. Direct-to-recipient delivery puts Progressive in the middle of email deliverability, recipient support ("I never got my card"), and the provability question Gord has been the right voice on — what happens when a recipient says they didn't receive a card we can prove was sent and opened. None of that's a no. It's a "let's scope it honestly the first time so the answer holds up six months in." Redemption reporting is harder still — most merchants don't expose redemption data to third parties — but the vault we've been narrowing in on internally is the foundation that makes the *good* version of that possible per merchant.

That's the other piece worth mentioning: this client conversation lines up cleanly with the secure card vault recommendation I've been drafting for when you're back. The vault is the right foundation under any direct-to-recipient delivery for the food bank-type clients, so if this one becomes a real engagement it would pull forward thinking we were already doing. I'd love to walk you through the refreshed recommendation alongside this.

Want me to reach out to them directly to set up a time, or would you rather make the introduction and we join from there?

Best,
Spencer
