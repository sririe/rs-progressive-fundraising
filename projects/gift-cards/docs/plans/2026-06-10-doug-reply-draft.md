Hi Doug,

Thanks again for these — and for the sales mix numbers, which genuinely shaped our answers. With 90% of digital volume on Walmart, Loblaws, and Amazon, Phase 1 needs to be specific about those three by name, and it now is.

Attached are our detailed responses to all twelve questions. A few headlines so you, James, and Lloyd know what you're looking at before you dig in:

**What "customer-ready file creation" means, merchant by merchant.** For Walmart, the vault prepares the activation work file, the activation step itself stays exactly as Lloyd runs it today, and the activated card data comes back in through a controlled import — then the vault produces the final customer file. For Loblaws, it's the approved spreadsheet of allocated card URLs. For Amazon, it's the PDF/ZIP package the current process produces. In every case the goal is the one you set: nobody copying card numbers between spreadsheets or running scripts.

**Security is built around one rule: full card numbers never appear on screen.** Card data is encrypted in the vault, and the only way to get full values out is a password-protected export that only your named Admin and Operations staff can run — and every export is logged: who, when, and for which order. That means the audit trail for sensitive access is complete by design, which we think is the right posture for cash-equivalent data.

**Where your decisions come in.** The responses flag a short list of items for you to confirm — who gets which access level, how exported files are handled after download, and the acceptance group for the "anyone in the office can fulfill" test. We've proposed a sensible default for each so this is a confirm-or-adjust exercise, not homework.

**One ask to get kickoff right.** The first project step is a validation pass where we confirm each merchant format against real materials. The responses include a single checklist of what we need from your side — mostly files Lloyd already has (templates, sample inputs, known-good outputs, the Walmart activation materials). A raw folder dump is perfect; we'll sort and inventory it. Getting that started now means the build starts building, not hunting.

On support: November–December being 60% of your sales is exactly why we'd rather propose a proper support plan than quote a one-off emergency rate. We'll include a recommended support agreement alongside the updated SOW.

Happy to walk through any of this on a call with you, James, and Lloyd — often faster than email for these. And we'll get the National Zakat call scheduled; Stephanie will send some times.

Best,
Spencer
