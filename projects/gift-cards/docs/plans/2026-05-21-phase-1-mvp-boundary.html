Deeper context - Phase 1 scope

Secure card vault MVP boundary

This page defines what the first build should probably include, what should wait, and where the team needs to refine assumptions before pricing.

Purpose

Stabilize fulfillment first.

Phase 1 should reduce the risk in digital inventory and fulfillment without trying to replace every adjacent system at once.

Standalone

Useful without Phase 2.

The vault should be valuable even if the Formidable Forms replacement and customer portal come later.

Foundation

Sets up the roadmap.

The same inventory, order, card-data, and delivery records should support portal, recipient delivery, and merchant-branded portals later.

Likely in Phase 1

Order queue and status

A place for paid digital orders to appear, move through fulfillment, and show whether they are waiting, in progress, ready, sent, failed, or blocked.

Digital card inventory

Track card data by merchant, denomination, source, status, and order assignment. This includes URLs, PINs, claim codes, card numbers, PDFs, and generated files where applicable.

Merchant-aware workflows

Guide staff through the correct steps for merchant-provided cards, Progressive-generated PDFs, and Walmart activation.

Customer-ready files and emails

Create or pre-stage the files and email copy Progressive sends today, including the separate password email where required.

Order history

Record who processed the order, what was created, what was sent, what was skipped, and what failed.

Access and cleanup rules

Define who can see card data and how long generated files, ZIPs, spreadsheets, and PDFs stay in the system.

Likely out of Phase 1

Not in first build Why it waits Where it belongs
Replacing Formidable Forms The current ordering layer can stay in place while fulfillment is stabilized. Phase 2: order platform and portal
QuickBooks integration Invoicing remains a business-rule-heavy workflow that should not block the vault. Phase 2 or later, depending on effort
Direct recipient delivery This changes Progressive's service and support posture, not just the software. Phase 3: recipient delivery service
Redemption reporting Merchant data availability varies, and most merchants do not expose redemption status cleanly. Phase 3: reporting where possible
Full SystemOne replacement SystemOne can remain an external step unless API availability changes the economics. Later optimization
White-label merchant portals These should wait until merchant partnerships justify the investment. Phase 3: merchant-branded portals

Questions that affect pricing

Merchant data

How consistent is inventory?

We need to know how similar the merchant spreadsheets, card files, and templates really are before estimating configuration effort.

Walmart

How much can V1 automate?

Walmart activation may need to stay partially manual until PowerShell, portal access, and reconciliation behavior are characterized.

Security

What is the agreed posture?

The difference between local, web, and hybrid architecture changes build cost, support cost, and future roadmap compatibility.

How to use this in review

Implementation review

Challenge the boundary.

Identify anything listed as in-scope that is too large for V1, and anything excluded that would make the vault unusable.

Project review

Turn scope into proposal language.

Confirm which items need to be client promises, which are assumptions, and which should stay as roadmap items.
